## Audience
Teams spread across time zones who want to see when colleagues’ working hours overlap, and people who also use Heinzelmännchen from a terminal.

## Outcome
Show how World Clock brings the same fictional team’s local times into the app and CLI, and how a later time can be checked before scheduling. The clock is included in the standard app; this story helps readers decide whether to use and configure it (`README.md`, `pyproject.toml`).

## Scenes
1. **World clock:** The light app view combines the globe and zone list with Today’s working-zone count. The markers represent time-zone names, not people (`web/src/ClockPanel.tsx`, `web/src/messages.ts`).
2. **Team overview:** The dark app view shows the Today metric, sidebar ticker, and primary footer clock together. These are separate Core-rendered contributions backed by the clock operation (`src/heinzel_clock/__init__.py`: `at_work`, `zones_ticker`, `primary_zone`).
3. **Footer detail:** Open the primary clock to inspect all configured zones and their workday countdowns. The primary badge and state icons depend on host support; older hosts use a text designation (`README.md`, `_detail` in `src/heinzel_clock/__init__.py`).
4. **Phone:** Show the actual World Clock view at phone width, including its globe and zone list. Capture only if the useful content is visible; do not substitute a desktop crop.
5. **CLI:** Show the rendered `hz clock --at` result for the same fictional team, including zones, local times, working hours, and working status.
6. **Native terminal, current example:** Show the 15:30 UTC operation in the app terminal: Berlin and New York are working; Los Angeles begins in 30 minutes.
7. **Native terminal, overlap example:** Run the same operation at 16:30 UTC, when all three fictional zones are working. This is a separate parameter case, not a feature UI screenshot. The existing plan says the terminal expands over Today, so do not claim the app clock is visible alongside it.
